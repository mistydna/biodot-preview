Research

Five connected themes, all aimed at better diagnosis and treatment for people with ALS.

Bioinformatics

We build open tools that take raw sequencing data to answers clinicians can use. They include DNAscan, a pipeline for analysing, annotating and visualising sequencing data on an ordinary computer, and methods for calling clinically reportable structural variants. Our code is shared openly on GitHub.

Biomarkers

A reliable biomarker tells you who has ALS, how quickly it is progressing and whether a treatment is working. We develop protein-based biomarkers for neurodegeneration, and have helped validate plasma neurofilament light as a diagnostic marker across multiple centres.

Drug discovery

Genetic evidence points to the genes and pathways that drive disease, and drugs aimed at genetically supported targets are more likely to succeed. We combine genetics, big data and AI to find new targets for ALS and dementia and to rank existing ones. Ahmad leads the Drug Discovery and Trials Optimisation working group in the DEMON network.

Omics

Through Project MinE, we analyse whole genomes from thousands of people with ALS. Our structural variant analysis of 6,500 genomes linked changes in C9orf72, VCP and ERBB4 to ALS risk and to how the disease presents. Ahmad chairs Project MinE's Trans-Ancestral Genetics working group, so that ALS genetics reflects people of every ancestry, not only those of European descent.

Trials

We use what we learn about genetics and progression to make trials smaller, faster and fairer. Our work includes showing at which stage riluzole prolongs survival in ALS, standardising King's clinical staging, and VariantSurvival, a tool for finding genotypes linked to treatment response.

BIODOT Lab, led by Dr Ahmad Al Khleifat in the Department of Basic and Clinical Neuroscience, King's College London.
